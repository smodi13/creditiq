# Investment case — Brandywine Realty Trust (BDN): hypothetical $400M first-lien term loan

> Independent research and underwriting exercise. **The loan does not exist.** Its size, pricing, floor, collateral pool and purchase price are hypothetical analyst assumptions; the issuer's financials and debt stack are real (SEC filings). Not a real transaction and not an investment by Fortress or anyone else. Figures come from the audited CreditIQ model (`models/CreditIQ_BDN_Model.xlsx`, web route `/companies/bdn`).

## 1. Executive summary
**Question:** would a lender be paid enough to provide a $400M first-lien term loan (SOFR + 525 bp, 1% floor, bought at 98) to an office REIT with $1.9B of debt maturing 2027–2029 and ~1.7x EBITDA/interest, assuming the loan is secured by a pool equal to 30% of the issuer's asset value?

**Answer: HOLD.** The base IRR is **9.6%** (9.9% on quarterly coupon timing) against a 10.0% hurdle; the probability-weighted IRR is **9.3%** (9.6% contractual). Recovery on the loan is **100% in every scenario** — but that is a property of the *assumed* collateral pool and the assumed asset values, not independent evidence of low risk, and the model's own stress shows where it breaks (§8). The three drivers: (1) **yield vs hurdle** — S+525 at 98 leaves ~9.7% YTM, below the 10% hurdle; (2) **collateral assumptions** — the whole recovery case rests on the 30% pool and a 7.5% cap rate; (3) **issuer refinancing** — 50% probability-weighted payment default of the issuer in the downside scenarios, with the base case itself cash-flow-negative and dependent on $200M of asset sales.

## 2. Company and instrument
**Actual (10-K FY2025, 10-Q 2026-06-30):** self-administered office/life-science REIT concentrated in Philadelphia and Austin; revenue $484.5M (FY2025), $505.5M, $514.7M; reported NOI $299.3M (FY2025); LTM Jun-26 NOI $301.5M, EBITDA (NOI − G&A) ~$265M; interest expense $135.0M in FY2025 and $82.8M in H1-2026 (+29% on H1-2025 as 8.3–8.9% notes replaced ~4% debt); cash $37.9M; core occupancy 88.3% (31 Dec 2025); impairments $63.4M (FY2025), $11.9M (H1-2026); $232.9M of assets held for sale at 2026-06-30. The company states it was in compliance with its financial covenants at 2026-06-30.
**Actual debt at 2026-06-30 (10-Q Note 7), principal $2,624.934M:** secured $147.3M (Avira term loan $90.0M due 2033 swapped to ~5.81%; 3151 Market C-PACE $57.3M at 7.31% due 2054); unsecured $2,477.6M (revolver $149.0M drawn of $600M, availability **$425.5M** net of $25.5M letters of credit, extended to 30 Dec 2026 with one more six-month option; $250M term loan due June 2027 swapped to fixed; 3.95% notes $450M Nov-2027; 8.30% $350M Mar-2028; 8.875% $550M Apr-2029; 4.55% $350M Oct-2029; 6.125% $300M Jan-2031; trust-preferred $78.6M 2035). In June 2026 the $178M 3025 JFK construction loan was retired. Credit-agreement tests: leverage ≤0.60, fixed-charge coverage ≥1.5x, secured debt ≤0.40 of total asset value, unencumbered cash flow / unsecured interest >1.75x.

**Hypothetical (analyst) — all labelled HYPOTHETICAL INVESTMENT TERMS in the app:** $400M senior secured term loan (we hold $100M), SOFR + 5.25% with a 1.00% SOFR floor, 1% annual amortisation, matures Jun-2031, **price 98**, 0.5% fee, call protection 102/101/par; proceeds repay the $149M revolver draw and the $250M term loan (pro forma debt $2,625.9M); **pool = 30% of recovery value**; asset value = LTM NOI ÷ **7.5% cap rate** + book value of CIP, land and JV investments ($4,518M); Avira/C-PACE collateral at an assumed 60% LTV; hypothetical maintenance covenants (net leverage ≤10.5x→9.5x, liquidity ≥$100M).

## 3. Thesis
A secured lender into a maturity wall is paid S+525 (all-in ~10.5% with SOFR at 3.9–5.25 bp floors), 2 points of OID and amortisation, and sits first in line on a ring-fenced pool; unsecured paper has largely left the office market, so a secured bridge is scarce capital. The loan extends the runway on $849M of 2027 maturities.

## 4. Capital structure
Rank 1: hypothetical $400M term loan (pool 30%), Avira $90M (pool 3.3%), C-PACE $57.3M (pool 2.1%) → rank 2: revolver (undrawn pro forma, $574.5M commitment net of LCs), five unsecured note series ($2.0B) → rank 3: trust preferred ($78.6M). Maturity wall (pro forma): $0 in LTM Jun-27E, $800M in 2028, $550M in 2029 (the model's periods end in June). LTM net leverage 9.78x, coverage 1.72x; credit score 45.7 (Elevated band starts at 45; an ordinal score, not a default probability).

## 5. Financial analysis
Base case: revenue −3% then +2%/yr, NOI-based EBITDA margin 53% → 54%, capex + leasing ~22% of revenue, "other cash items" (distributions) 11% of revenue; asset sales $200M in period 1. **Cumulative five-period FCF is −$436M** even in the base case (capex/leasing and distributions exceed cash from operations), so liquidity relies on the revolver and asset sales; net leverage drifts from 9.6x to 10.0x and coverage stays 1.34–1.47x. Downside: revenue −6%, −3%, 0, +1, +1; margin 50% → 49.5%; asset value −15%; SOFR +150 bp; refinancing rate 10%; peak net leverage 12.0x, minimum coverage 1.18x, 7 refinancing failures, issuer payment default in 2028. Severe: revenue −10%…, margin 46% → 44%, asset value −30%, SOFR +300 bp, peak leverage 15.8x, coverage 0.90x, default in 2028.

## 6. Base case (50%)
Performing, exit at par after three years: −98.49 (price 98 + 0.5% fee), +9.15, +9.00, +108.75 → **IRR 9.58% annual / 9.91% contractual (quarterly coupons), MOIC 1.29x**. Entry price at which the base IRR reaches the 10% hurdle: 97.0 (vs 98 assumed); the expected-cash-flow IRR needs ≤ 96.4.

## 7. Downside case (35%)
Issuer payment default in 2028 (refinancing of the 2027–28 maturities fails the market-access test at 9.5x / 1.5x). Coupons stop after 2028; the loan is repaid from the pledged pool a period later: proceeds 102.6 (par plus 3 months accrued interest) → **IRR 8.74% / 8.99% contractual, MOIC 1.26x**. Severe (15%): default 2028, same mechanics → IRR 9.94% / 10.27%.
**Read this carefully:** the severe IRR is *higher* than the base IRR. That is an artefact of the 100%-recovery assumption, not a feature: a defaulted floating-rate loan that is repaid in full plus accrued interest keeps the higher coupons earned under the +300 bp shock and exits early at 103 instead of 100. It is evidence that the collateral assumption is doing all the work, not that default is harmless.

## 8. Recovery — and where it stops being 100%
**Method:** value = MAX(going concern = distressed multiple × event EBITDA, liquidation = asset value × (1 − decline) × 85% realization) + cash, less 4% costs; the loan is paid first from its own pool (**30% × net value**, capped at its claim), any shortfall joining general creditors at the lowest rank. Base/downside/severe recovery value: $3,990M / $3,264M / $2,688M (liquidation basis in all three) → pool $1.15B / $0.94B / $0.77B vs a claim of ~$410M → 100%.
**What the 30% means:** it is the share of the *issuer's total recovery value* ring-fenced for this loan. It is not a verified list of properties, not a share of NOI, and not a share of property count; it also (mechanically) applies 30% to the book value of CIP, land and JV investments.
**Stress (asset-based floor, `Recovery` → Collateral coverage stress; model-computed, reproduced in the Excel Sensitivities tab):**
- Total asset value needed for 100% recovery: **$1.63B vs $4.52B base — a 64% decline** (at the Severe 85% realization factor). Equivalent: cap rate **26.5%** at unchanged NOI, or NOI **−72%** at a 7.5% cap rate. The grid shows 100% at cap rates up to 14% with NOI −40%, falling to 96% at 14% / −50%.
- **Pool share is the sensitive assumption:** with a 10% pool the loan already recovers only 92% at 85% realization, 76% at 70%; at 20% it recovers 87% / 54% at 40% / 25% realization; 30% needs realization below ~31% before recovery falls (81% at 25%).
- **Loan balance:** at 2x the claim (a $800M loan) recovery is 83% at −40% asset value and 55% at −60%; at 3x it is 92% even at today's values.
- Realization costs: 100% recovery holds down to 40% realization at a 30% pool.
So 100% recovery persists under materially harsher cap-rate and NOI assumptions **only if the 30% pool and the asset-value basis are right**. Cut the pool to 10% and the answer changes immediately. Distinguish the loan's protection from the issuer's complete capital structure: in the severe-case waterfall the trust-preferred notes recover 25% and equity is wiped out.

## 9. Risks
1. **Collateral assumptions** — pool share, cap rate and the inclusion of non-operating book values are not evidenced in the filings (appraisals needed).
2. **Issuer refinancing/liquidity** — $849M of 2027 maturities (before the hypothetical loan; $450M of notes fall in Nov-2027) and a base-case FCF deficit; secured-debt capacity under the unsecured indentures and the 40% secured cap would need waivers (the loan takes secured debt from $147M to $547M).
3. **Rate and refinancing cost** — 8.3–8.9% notes are replacing 4% debt; interest expense +29% YoY.
4. **Office values/NOI** — impairments $63M (FY2025) and $12M (H1-26); occupancy 88%.
5. **Model artefact risk** — 100% recovery in all scenarios, severe IRR above base IRR.

## 10. Protections
First-lien on a ring-fenced pool; ~29.5% pool LTV ($400M ÷ $1.36B) at the assumed values; floating rate with 1% floor; 1% amortisation and OID; hypothetical maintenance covenants (minimum liquidity and leverage); only $147M of other secured debt.

## 11. Recommendation — HOLD (rule output)
Core tests: base IRR 9.6% vs ≥10.0% **fail**; expected-cash-flow IRR 9.3% vs ≥10.0% **fail**; downside IRR 8.7% vs ≥0% pass; downside recovery 100% vs ≥90% pass; base refinancing pass. Secondary: yield 9.7% vs ≥8.5% pass; net leverage 9.8x vs ≤10.5x pass; **covenant headroom −10.5% (proxy credit-agreement tests, base case, minimum) fails** — note the company itself states it is in compliance; the proxy tests use the model's asset value and EBITDA, not the credit agreement's definitions; severe-case principal protection passes (100 vs 98); **high-priority diligence fails** (6 open). Two core failures → HOLD. A purchase price of about 97.0 or lower (vs 98) would clear the 10% hurdle in the base case, and about 96.4 on the probability-weighted measure; independent appraisals of the pledged properties are the most valuable diligence item.

## 12. Key limitations
The loan, its pricing, the 30% pool, the 7.5% cap rate and collateral shares are hypothetical; the filings do not disclose property values or the unencumbered pool; recovery value takes the higher of going-concern and liquidation values; the stress is an asset-based floor on principal at close; post-default periods in the projection are mechanical (cash goes negative in default scenarios); annual operating periods (quarterly coupons only in the contractual-timing IRR); disclosed covenants are tested on proxy definitions; revolver extension to Jun-2027 is assumed exercised; scenario probabilities are not calibrated; the 10-Q's later-period events are not reflected.
